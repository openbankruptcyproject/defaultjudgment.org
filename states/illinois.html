Illinois Default Judgment Vacatur Rules

Illinois has one of the tighter windows in the country to vacate a default judgment. The primary vacatur rule (735 ILCS 5/2-1301, 5/2-1401) imposes a 30 days (Section 2-1301) / 2 years (2-1401) deadline for most enumerated grounds. After that window closes, only narrow grounds remain (void judgment, jurisdictional defect, or equitable relief).

RuleIllinois Standard
Primary rule citation735 ILCS 5/2-1301, 5/2-1401

Window to vacate (ordinary)30 days (Section 2-1301) / 2 years (2-1401)
Window for void judgment30 years (void)

Illinois has two distinct windows: 30-day motion to vacate under 2-1301 with near-automatic relief; 2-1401 petition up to 2 years requires due diligence + meritorious defense.

How Illinois Courts Evaluate Your Motion

The 30-day 2-1301 window is near-automatic; miss it and 2-1401 standard is much harder.

The universal thread across most Illinois cases: you must show both a procedural excuse (why you did not answer) and a meritorious defense (why you should have won). One without the other rarely succeeds.

  • Excusable neglect. Genuine accident, illness, or service failure - not mere forgetfulness or choice to ignore.
  • Meritorious defense. SOL, lack of contract, wrong defendant, paid debt, or FDCPA violation. See the full list.
  • Prompt motion. Illinois courts penalize delay. File immediately after learning of the judgment.
  • No prejudice to plaintiff. The longer you wait, the more the plaintiff has relied on the judgment.

Debt Collector Failure to Appear - Illinois Rules

If the collector or plaintiff fails to appear at a required hearing in Illinois, you can often move for dismissal with prejudice. The cure rules flip in this scenario:

  • Status hearing no-show. Most Illinois courts enter a dismissal for lack of prosecution after one or two missed appearances.
  • Garnishment hearing no-show. The writ is quashed and any withheld wages may be ordered returned.
  • Evidence hearing no-show. If the collector cannot produce a live witness with personal knowledge of the account, the court may sua sponte dismiss.

Many junk-debt buyers lose on failure-to-appear alone because they cannot produce a live witness with knowledge of the original account. Always demand a live witness and attend every hearing.

Improper Service in Illinois - The Strongest Ground

Service defects are the most common - and strongest - ground to vacate in Illinois. When service was never properly made, the default judgment is void (not merely voidable), which means it can be attacked at any time under most state rules, not just within the ordinary vacatur window. Even so, file promptly once you learn the judgment exists; unexplained delay only invites arguments against you.

Common service defects in Illinois:

  • "Sewer service" - process server lies about delivery and you never receive the summons.
  • Service on wrong address after you moved.
  • Substitute service on someone not authorized to accept (e.g., minor child, ex-spouse, neighbor).
  • Publication service without exhausting personal-service attempts.
  • Service on a co-defendant rather than you directly.

See improper-service defense deep dive for the affidavit template and evidentiary burden.

Extrinsic vs Intrinsic Fraud in Illinois

Illinois distinguishes extrinsic fraud (cognizable as void-judgment attack, no time limit in most states) from intrinsic fraud (subject to the ordinary vacatur window).

  • Extrinsic fraud - fraud on the court that prevented you from presenting your defense. Example: the plaintiff lied about your address to prevent service.
  • Intrinsic fraud - fraud in the evidence or pleadings presented. Example: the plaintiff attached a forged contract. Subject to the 30 days (Section 2-1301) / 2 years (2-1401) window in Illinois.

Extrinsic fraud is the stronger path in Illinois because it is generally not subject to the ordinary time bar.

When Bankruptcy Resolves the Underlying Debt

If you cannot vacate the default judgment in Illinois, bankruptcy often remains viable:

  • Chapter 7 discharges the underlying debt, making the judgment unenforceable as to you personally.
  • Chapter 13 treats the judgment as a general unsecured claim paid pro rata through the plan.
  • Judgment lien avoidance under 11 U.S.C. Section 522(f) strips judgment liens that impair your homestead exemption.
